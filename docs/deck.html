    The moment

    An invoice for $340,000 lands. The crew is due back Monday. Her tool answers in three seconds with a tidy paragraph — and she opens the accounting system and rebuilds the number by hand anyway.

    A composite of two documented general contractors: a 25–30 PM firm whose monthly forecasting was a multi-day export-to-Excel-and-back exercise, and a ten-office firm where a $10 million project was linked to the wrong job, could not be reversed, and ran off-book for months. Meridian Builders is a composite name, not a real company.

    • She sees the real financial picture once a month — by then the mistake is weeks old.

    Decision 03 — The workflow

    Four checks. Three answers. Confidence is a count.

    The rule that makes it honest

    A check that could not run is never counted as passed. It leaves the denominator and routes to Comment. A model reporting "95% confident" has produced another fabricated receipt — this number is checks passed over checks applicable, and every term is nameable.

    Decision 04 — What we build

    Four errors we catch, and what each one costs when we don't.

    Check 1

    Intake: the invoice is incomplete

    Missing PO, missing period, unreadable amount. It enters the pipeline anyway and every check downstream answers a question about a document nobody read.

    $25,000/ yr

    Mistral OCR 4.1 reads the document and reports which required fields are absent — by name, before anything else runs.

    Check 2

    The wrong vendor gets paid

    Two records, one company. ACME Concrete and ACME Concrete, Inc. are separate rows with separate payment histories.

    $340,000/ incident

    Candidate set must resolve to exactly one vendor. If it doesn't, the system halts and asks — it never picks.

    Check 3

    The same vendor is paid twice

    The duplicate search runs against one spelling, finds nothing, and reports clean. The second payment goes out.

    $272,000/ yr

    Search every spelling resolved by check 2, against PO, amount and period. A prior paid row is a hard deny.

    Check 4

    The job is dead and the payment isn't

    The ERP closed the job, or never sanctioned it. The invoice still gets approved against a budget line that no longer accepts charges.

    $10,000,000/ incident

    Resolve status through the FMS ID → PID join. Undefined status is not a pass — it routes to Comment.

    Basis. $10,000,000 and $68,000 are named incidents in published contractor case studies — a project linked to the wrong job and run off-book for months, and a double-billed material order caught by hand. $272,000/yr is 4 × the $68,000 unit at the upper end of a 1–4-per-year rate. $340,000 is the invoice on this demo. $25,000/yr is manual error-resolution time — a reported 5–10 hours per week across an accounting team, at a loaded rate. Everything except the two named incidents is an estimate.

    No public dataset contains a contractor's invoice ledger — contractors do not publish who they paid and when. Three of four checks need one. So we generate it, and we say so on stage rather than implying the invoices are real.

    Decision 06 — The proof measured

    Two failures, both already in the data, both invisible to a green pipeline.

    Queried live against fb86-vt7u, reporting period 202605. Not estimated.

    Failure one — the key fans out

    207of 3,063 projects carry more than one FMS ID
    382surplus rows a naive join invents
    $9.93Bsits on those projects
    14FMS IDs on the worst single project (pid 2845)

    Failure two — the join drops a third of the money and says nothing

    2,356rows with no project ID (2,343 distinct FMS IDs)
    $64.84Bof budget on those rows
    31.2%of the period's dollars unjoinable to a project
    $0errors raised when this happens
    The one that matters

    Fan-out double-counts loudly — a total that grows is a total someone questions. The silent drop is the dangerous one: an inner join discards $64.84B and returns a smaller, tidier, entirely credible number. Neither prior handoff had this second failure. The data did.

    Read this before trusting the ranking

    Only criterion 1 is derived from counts. Four of the five are declared judgment. And the ordering of criteria 2 and 3 — Isha 0.85 above Shivam 0.70 — was supplied as a priority, not derived from evidence, yet it is the only thing separating first place from second on a 0.65 margin. The ranking is a useful instrument, not an oracle. It is one of the reasons the selected project was chosen on evidence the ranking never scored.

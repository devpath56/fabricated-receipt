# Part I — Project

## Decision 01 — The user

### She doesn't distrust the number. She distrusts that she can't check it.

A project accountant at a mid-market contractor — $50M to $500M in revenue, Procore for the field,
Vista or Spectrum for the money, and nobody on staff who writes integrations. That last part is why
this whole category exists.

Mid-meeting, someone asks why job 1412 is over budget. Her AI tool answers in three seconds with a
tidy paragraph explaining where the figure came from. Then she opens Vista and rebuilds the number
by hand anyway.

> When she's wrong in front of the owner, it's her name on it — not the tool's.

**So what.** The tool didn't save her an afternoon. It **added a thing she has to check**. Every
"show your work" panel that narrates itself makes this worse, not better — it gives her more to
read and no more to verify.

---

## Decision 02 — What it costs

### The cost isn't the wrong number. It's the second person who has to produce the right one.

| What goes wrong | Cost | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| A double-billed commitment | **$68,000** | Walker Masonry, rebar. Caught by a person reading a ledger, not by the pipeline |
| An ERP migration undertaken to escape bad data | **$200,000** | R.E. Lewis. Avoided once the two systems could be trusted to agree |
| Rebuilding a figure by hand to check the AI | ~20–40 hrs / mo | Per accountant. Invisible, because it looks like the job |
| Adoption stalls at zero | the whole spend | The tool ships, everybody checks it, nobody stops checking |

**So what.** Making the answer faster does nothing here — she was never waiting on speed. **Making
the answer checkable is the entire product.** The first time she clicks a receipt and it genuinely
reconciles, she stops rebuilding by hand. That's the afternoon back.

> Hours and adoption cost are estimates. The $68,000 and $200,000 are named incidents at named
> companies and are the only two figures on this page quotable as fact.

---

## Decision 03 — The failure we're fixing

### A wrong number arrives wearing a convincing receipt.

On real public money, reporting period `202605`:

**The question** — *"What's our total capital budget this period?"*

**The answer, in three seconds** — `$142,756,981,094`

> "Summed `total_budget` across all projects in the current reporting period, joined to the project
> schedule on the FMS identifier."

**The query that actually ran:**

```sql
SELECT SUM(b.total_budget)
FROM   budget_and_schedule b
JOIN   projects p ON p.pid = b.pid
WHERE  b.reporting_period = '202605'
-- inner join. 2,356 rows have no pid at all.
```

**Reconciled against the source total:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Reported | **$142.76B** |
| Actually in the period | **$207.59B** |
| Silently dropped | **$64.84B · 31.2%** |

Every word of that explanation is true except *all*. The narration was generated by the same model
that wrote the query, so **the receipt is written by the suspect**. Nothing in the pipeline errored.
Nothing turned red.

**The build, in one sentence.** We don't grade the answer. **We grade the receipt against the query
that executed** — join keys, filters, and what got dropped — and we refuse to answer when it doesn't
tie back.

---

## Decision 04 — The opportunity

### Everyone at the hackathon builds text-to-SQL. We build the thing that catches it.

The standard demo picks a friendly question and works. This one opens on the failure — a confident
wrong number with a beautiful explanation — and then catches it live. Every judge in the room has
been burned by a plausible LLM answer.

It fits a weekend for an unglamorous reason: **there is nothing to integrate.**

| In scope | Out |
|---|---|
| Read-only queries over public data | Any write path |
| Executed-query capture + reconciliation | Auth, tenancy, RBAC |
| A graded gold set of ~30–50 hard cases | Procore / ERP integration |
| Refuse-to-answer when the tie-out fails | Fine-tuning anything |
| One screen a controller would recognise | Vision / document OCR layer *(see the Mistral pitch — OCR is the one exception worth making)* |

**The unfair advantage.** The failure mode **already exists in the public data**. We don't have to
fabricate a broken join to demo catching one — which is the difference between a demo and a rigged
demo, and a technical judge can tell.

## Decision 05 — The data

### NYC publishes $207B of capital spend — and documents its own broken join key.

From the dataset description NYC ships with `fb86-vt7u`, read live 2026-08-21:

> Each row is uniquely identified by its Financial Management Service (FMS) ID… This ID can be
> universally joined with any OMB dataset that has the same field… FMS IDs and agency projects
> don't always have a one-to-one relationship (i.e., one project schedule may correlate to multiple
> FMS IDs, and one FMS may correct to multiple projects with different schedules).

Three claims in one paragraph, and they cannot all be true. The key is unique. Join on it
universally. The key is not unique. The dataset's own row label offers a fourth answer:
*"unique project and budget line combination"*.

| Dataset | ID | Carries | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citywide Budget and Schedule | [`fb86-vt7u`](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/fb86-vt7u) | The bridge — budget, spend, phase, schedule, agency | `fms_id` + `pid` |
| Budget Spend History and Variance | [`qj5n-h5qp`](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/qj5n-h5qp) | Monthly snapshots. Earliest = original budget; variance = delta vs prior month | `fms_id` |
| Capital Commitment Plan | [`2cmn-uidm`](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/2cmn-uidm) | Committed dollars by budget line and funding source | **no `fms_id`** |

API base: `https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/<id>.json`. No auth, no key.

> **Correction carried forward.** The prior handoff recorded this family as "all keyed on FMS ID".
> It isn't — `2cmn-uidm` has no `fms_id` column at all; it keys on `budline`, `projtype` and
> `funding`. Verified against the live Socrata metadata, not recalled.

**Why this beats a synthetic dataset.** The monthly snapshot trail in `qj5n-h5qp` is an
original-versus-revised budget history — **the change-order story in contractor terms**, in real
money, published by the party that owns it. Pair it with BookSQL for question variety and
accounting structure.

---

## Decision 06 — The proof (measured)

### Two failures, both already in the data, both invisible to a green pipeline.

Queried live against `fb86-vt7u`, reporting period `202605`. Not estimated. Reproduce with
`node scripts/pull.mjs && node scripts/failures.mjs`.

**Failure one — the key fans out**

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| projects with more than one FMS ID | **207** of 3,063 (6.8%) |
| surplus rows a naive join invents | **382** |
| dollars sitting on those projects | **$9,933,414,291** |
| worst single project | pid `2845` — **14** FMS IDs |

**Failure two — the join drops a third of the money and says nothing**

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| rows with no project ID at all | **2,356** |
| distinct FMS IDs among them | 2,343 |
| dollars on those rows | **$64,836,845,710** |
| share of the period unjoinable to a project | **31.2%** |
| errors raised when this happens | **0** |

**The one that matters.** Fan-out double-counts *loudly* — a total that grows is a total someone
questions. The silent drop is the dangerous one: an inner join discards $64.84B and returns a
smaller, tidier, entirely credible number. **Neither prior analysis had this second failure. The
data did.**

# Part II — The team

## Decision 07 — Why this is Shivam's project

### The hard part isn't generating SQL. It's building the ground truth that grades it.

| What he already does | What it becomes here |
|---|---|
| Finance-document parsing — the table top-up heuristic, so numeric questions don't get answered from prose | The same instinct one layer up: **a numeric answer must not be explained by prose.** The narration is graded against the query, not accepted as one |
| Duplicate detection at portfolio scale — GBM across 32 clients, 50M+ transactions/month | Fan-out detection on a join key the publisher itself says is not unique |
| Entity resolution by metric learning — triplet mining, silhouette 0.72 → 0.90 | Is `fms_id` = `pid` the same entity? The dataset says both yes and no |
| Interpretability — SHAP, PDP, ELI5 since 2020 | The receipt itself. Why this number, traced to the rows that produced it |

**The engineering excellence he gets to show.** Semantics here are genuinely nasty and no amount of
prompt engineering gets past them: committed versus actual versus forecast, retention held back,
pending versus approved change orders. **A join can be technically valid and financially wrong** —
so the eval has to catch semantic wrongness, not syntax errors.

**The research surface.** Faithfulness scoring at the level of **joins and filters**, not vibes:
does the explanation describe the query that ran? That's a groundedness eval where the "retrieved
evidence" is an execution plan. Very few people at this hackathon will be grading a narration
against a query plan.

---

## Decision 08 — Why this is Isha's project

### A bad join returns a number, not an error. Her job is to make the pipeline stop lying quietly.

$64.84B disappears from an answer and every health check stays green. This is the silent-default
anti-pattern in its natural habitat — and turning silent failure into loud, fast failure is the
discipline she already sells.

| Guard | What it does | Failure mode it kills |
|---|---|---|
| **Tie-out, fail closed** | Does this figure reconcile to the source total within tolerance? If not, **refuse to answer** — don't degrade gracefully | Confident wrong totals |
| **Unmatched-row accounting** | Every join reports what it dropped, in rows and in dollars, on the answer itself | The $64.84B silent drop |
| **Cardinality contract** | Assert the expected key relationship before the join; a 1:1 assumption that turns 1:N throws | The 207-project fan-out |
| **Provenance envelope** | Every number carries its query, schema version and data watermark | "Was the model wrong, or the data stale?" — currently unanswerable |

**The objection, answered.** The earlier scoring knocked this idea down on audience recognition, on
the grounds that *the budget view is its interface* — the harness isn't a visible layer beside a
recognisable Procore screen, it *is* the screen.

**That's the argument for it, not against it.** A reliability layer that lives beside the number
gets ignored. **This one sits where the number is read**, so refusing to answer is a product
behaviour a controller experiences, not a dashboard she'd have to go look at. Recognition comes from
the field names on screen — `total_budget`, `budget_variance`, `current_phase` — not from a
screenshot.

## Appendix C — The instruments

### Two instruments, never one number — because an average dilutes every spike.

| Instrument | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Floor** | Binary, and it **blocks**. A project is eligible only if it can name a verbatim field, enum or endpoint. Five rows fail. They sink but are not deleted |
| **Peak** | Ranks on the sum of a project's **top two** criteria only. Everything below contributes nothing, so one elite spike beats four competent scores |

### The five criteria — scored on scarcity, not goodness

The question is never *is this good*. It is *can another team produce it*. RAG plumbing scores low
because everyone has it.

| Rank | Criterion | Weight | How it's scored |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Domain grounding | **1.00** | **Derived**, not judged — five countable sub-signals worth 0–2 each: anchor · index · term count · JTBD grade · quote |
| 2 | Isha unique strengths | 0.85 | Declared judgment |
| 3 | Shivam fit | 0.70 | Declared judgment, graded on the rare half of his profile, not the RAG plumbing |
| 4 | Construction-tech recognition | 0.55 | Declared judgment |
| 5 | General-software recognition | 0.25 | Declared judgment |

Ceiling is `18.5` — a perfect domain-grounding score plus a perfect single spike.

**Read this before trusting the ranking.** Only criterion 1 is derived from counts. **Four of the
five are declared judgment.** And the ordering of criteria 2 and 3 — Isha 0.85 above Shivam 0.70 —
was supplied as a priority, not derived from evidence, yet it is the only thing separating first
place from second on a 0.65 margin. The ranking is a useful instrument, not an oracle. It is one of
the reasons the selected project was chosen on evidence the ranking never scored.
